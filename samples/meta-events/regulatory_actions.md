# Meta and the regulators: a timeline

A working note on the fines, settlements and rulings that have shaped Meta Platforms, Inc. since the Cambridge Analytica revelations. Amounts are as reported at the time; several decisions remain under appeal.

## 2018

On 17 March 2018 the Observer and the New York Times published the Cambridge Analytica story in London: data from up to 87 million Facebook profiles had been harvested through a quiz app. Three weeks later, on 10 April 2018, Mark Zuckerberg testified before the United States Senate in Washington, DC, for close to five hours.

The UK Information Commissioner's Office in Wilmslow imposed the maximum fine then available, 500,000 pounds, on 25 October 2018.

## 2019

On 24 July 2019 the Federal Trade Commission in Washington, DC announced a 5 billion dollar settlement, the largest privacy penalty in its history, together with a new privacy oversight structure for the board.

## 2020

On 9 December 2020 the FTC and a coalition of 46 states filed antitrust suits in Washington, DC, seeking to unwind the Instagram and WhatsApp acquisitions. The case went to trial in April 2025 and ended on 18 November 2025 with a ruling in Meta's favor.

## 2021

The UK Competition and Markets Authority in London fined Facebook 50.5 million pounds on 20 October 2021 for breaching an order during the Giphy review, and on 30 November 2021 ordered the Giphy acquisition unwound.

On 24 December 2021 a court in Moscow fined Meta about 27 million dollars for failing to remove banned content. In March 2022 Facebook and Instagram were blocked in Russia.

## 2023

The Irish Data Protection Commission in Dublin fined Meta 390 million euros on 4 January 2023 over the legal basis for personalized advertising, and on 22 May 2023 a record 1.2 billion euros over transfers of European user data to the United States.

Giphy was sold to Shutterstock for 53 million dollars on 23 May 2023 after the CMA's order.

## 2024

On 19 July 2024 Nigeria's Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission in Abuja fined Meta 220 million dollars over data practices on WhatsApp.

On 30 July 2024 the Texas Attorney General in Austin announced a 1.4 billion dollar settlement over facial recognition data, the largest privacy settlement obtained by a single state.

On 18 November 2024 the Competition Commission of India in New Delhi fined Meta 213 crore rupees over WhatsApp's 2021 privacy policy and ordered a five year ban on sharing WhatsApp data for advertising.

## 2025

On 23 April 2025 the European Commission in Brussels issued its first Digital Markets Act non-compliance decisions: 200 million euros for Meta's consent or pay model and 500 million euros for Apple. Meta appealed on 4 July 2025.

## 2026

In March 2026 a jury in Santa Fe, New Mexico returned a verdict of about 375 million dollars against Meta in the state attorney general's child safety case.
